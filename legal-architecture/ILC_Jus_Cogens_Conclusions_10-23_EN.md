# ILC Draft Conclusions on Peremptory Norms of General International Law (jus cogens)
## Conclusions 10–23 (A©tor source extract for CASE-MACHERET-1997-2026)

**A©TOR_KEY="# [⚖ A©tor Declaration]"**

### Conclusion 10 – Invalidity of a treaty conflicting with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens)
1. A treaty is void if, at the time of its conclusion, it conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens). Such a treaty does not create any rights or obligations.
2. An existing treaty becomes void and terminates if it conflicts with a new peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) that emerges after the conclusion of the treaty. The parties to such a treaty are released from any obligation further to perform the treaty.
3. In order to avoid a conflict with a peremptory norm of general international law, a treaty provision should, as far as possible, be interpreted in a way that renders it compatible with the peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).

### Conclusion 11 – Separability of treaty provisions conflicting with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens)
1. A treaty which, at the time of its conclusion, conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) is void in whole, and no part of the treaty may be separated or isolated.
2. A treaty which becomes void as a result of the emergence of a new peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) terminates in whole, except where:
   a) the provisions conflicting with the peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) are separable from the remainder of the treaty with regard to their application;
   b) the provisions conflicting with the peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) do not constitute an essential basis of the consent of the parties; and
   c) continued performance of the remainder of the treaty would not be unjust.

### Conclusion 12 – Elimination of the consequences of any acts performed on the basis of a void treaty
1. The parties to a treaty which is void as a result of conflict with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) at the time of the conclusion of that treaty have a legal obligation to eliminate the consequences of any act performed on the basis of a provision of the treaty that conflicts with the peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).
2. The termination of a treaty by reason of the emergence of a new peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) does not affect any right, obligation or legal situation of the parties created through the execution of the treaty prior to its termination, provided that such rights, obligations or legal situations are not themselves in conflict with the peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).

### Conclusion 13 – Effect of peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) on reservations to treaties
1. A reservation to a treaty provision that reflects a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) does not affect the binding character of that norm, which continues to apply.
2. A reservation may not exclude or modify the legal effect of a treaty in a manner contrary to a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).

### Conclusion 14 – Recommended procedure for the settlement of disputes relating to a conflict between a treaty and a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens)
1. Subject to the jurisdictional rules of the International Court of Justice, any dispute as to whether a treaty conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) should be submitted to the International Court of Justice for decision, unless the parties to the dispute agree to submit the dispute to arbitration.
2. Irrespective of paragraph 1, the fact that a dispute concerns a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) is not in itself a sufficient basis for the establishment of the Court’s jurisdiction without the necessary consent to jurisdiction in accordance with international law.

### Conclusion 15 – Consequences of peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) for customary international law
1. A rule of customary international law does not arise if it conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).
2. A rule of customary international law not of a jus cogens character ceases to exist if a new peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) emerges that conflicts with it.
3. Since peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) bind all subjects of international law, the persistent objector rule does not apply.

### Conclusion 16 – Consequences of peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) for unilateral acts
A unilateral act that conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) is void.

### Conclusion 17 – Consequences of peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) for binding resolutions of international organizations
1. Binding resolutions of international organizations, including resolutions of the United Nations Security Council, do not establish binding obligations if they conflict with a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).
2. Binding resolutions of international organizations, including resolutions of the United Nations Security Council, should, as far as possible, be interpreted in a manner consistent with peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens).

### Conclusion 18 – Relationship between peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) and obligations erga omnes
Peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) establish obligations erga omnes, the breach of which concerns all States.

### Conclusion 19 – Effect of peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) on circumstances precluding wrongfulness
1. No circumstance may be invoked to preclude the wrongfulness of an act that is not in conformity with an obligation arising under a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).
2. Paragraph 1 does not apply when the peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) is formed after the commission of the act.

### Conclusion 20 – Duty to cooperate
1. States shall cooperate to bring to an end through lawful means any serious breach of a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).
2. A serious breach of a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) is one that involves a gross or systematic failure to fulfil the obligation.
3. Cooperation contemplated by the present draft conclusion may be carried out through institutional mechanisms of cooperation or through ad hoc arrangements for interaction.

### Conclusion 21 – Duty of non-recognition and non-assistance
1. States shall not recognize as lawful a situation created by a serious breach of a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).
2. States shall not render aid or assistance in maintaining a situation created by a serious breach of a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).

### Conclusion 22 – Duty to exercise domestic jurisdiction over crimes prohibited by peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens)
1. States shall exercise jurisdiction over offences prohibited by peremptory norms of general international law (jus cogens) when the offences are committed by persons having the nationality of the State or on territory under its jurisdiction.
2. Paragraph 1 does not preclude the establishment of jurisdiction on any other basis permitted by national law.

### Conclusion 23 – Non-availability of official capacity and non-applicability of immunity ratione materiae
1. The fact that an offence prohibited by a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens) was committed by a person acting in an official capacity does not exclude criminal responsibility.
2. Immunity ratione materiae does not apply to any offence prohibited by a peremptory norm of general international law (jus cogens).
